\section*{Question 1: Tax Incidence}

The city of Vienna is considering a per-trip tax on ride-hailing services (Uber, Bolt). The market for rides is described by the inverse supply and demand curves
\[
P^S(Q) = 2 + 0.5Q, \qquad P^D(Q) = 20 - Q,
\]
where $Q$ is trips per hour and $P$ is the price in euros.

\qpart{a)} Find the equilibrium price and quantity \emph{before} any tax.

\qpart{b)} The city imposes a tax of \texteuro{}3 per trip, collected from the \textbf{drivers} (suppliers). Find the new equilibrium quantity, the price \emph{riders} pay, and the price \emph{drivers} keep after remitting the tax. How much of the \texteuro{}3 tax is borne by riders, and how much by drivers? In one sentence, connect your answer to the relative steepness (elasticity) of supply and demand in this market.

\section*{Question 2: Progressive Brackets, Average vs.\ Marginal Rates}

Austria's (simplified) income tax schedule has the following brackets:
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{@{}ll@{}}
\toprule
\textbf{Taxable income} & \textbf{Rate} \\
\midrule
Up to \texteuro{}13{,}539 & 0\% \\
\texteuro{}13{,}539 -- \texteuro{}21{,}992 & 20\% \\
\texteuro{}21{,}992 -- \texteuro{}36{,}458 & 30\% \\
\texteuro{}36{,}458 -- \texteuro{}70{,}365 & 40\% \\
\texteuro{}70{,}365 -- \texteuro{}104{,}859 & 48\% \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

Markus has taxable income of \texteuro{}50{,}000.

\qpart{a)} Calculate Markus's total tax bill by working up through the brackets. What is his \textbf{marginal} tax rate, and what is his \textbf{average} tax rate?

\qpart{b)} Next year, Markus's nominal income rises by 5\% (to \texteuro{}52{,}500), but prices have also risen by 5\%, so his \emph{real} purchasing power is exactly unchanged. If the brackets above stay fixed in nominal terms, explain in 2--3 sentences what happens to Markus's average tax rate, and name the phenomenon this illustrates. Briefly state how Austria's actual tax policy since 2023 addresses it.
